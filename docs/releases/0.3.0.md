Ara crashed after recordings, typed long dictation in the wrong order, and showed nothing at all when you launched it. This release is mostly about those three.

## Fixed

**Ara crashed after a recording.** A field report, then a segfault caught under lldb on a live daemon: `objc_msgSend` inside an AVFAudio block, on the `AVAudioIOUnit` queue, moments after `capture.stop()` returned. AVFAudio installs an AudioUnit property listener and dispatches blocks that message the engine; `engine.stop()` does not drain that queue, and removing the listener is AVFAudio's job rather than ours. Ara built a fresh `AVAudioEngine` for every recording and released it inside `stop()`, so every utterance left one freed engine for a queued block to find. The engine is now reused across recordings and a replaced one is held rather than dropped.

A second, related race went with it. The audio-configuration observer runs synchronously on an AVFoundation queue, and it only enqueued work — so a rebuild could start tearing the engine down while AVFoundation was still unwinding the notification through that same engine, which Apple's contract forbids. A Bluetooth input renegotiating A2DP/HFP posts these in bursts (three inside a two-second capture, measured), which is what made a latent race reproducible.

**Long dictation arrived with a piece moved to the end.** Synthesized keystrokes carry about 20 characters each, so a paragraph is delivered as a sequence of events — and the order they are committed in is not the order they were posted. Two captures, two different apps, same signature both times: one whole chunk overtaken by the rest and committed last.

```text
...everywhere, but not[ from VidNotes web. ]And it looked like...
                      └──── moved here ────────────────────────┐
...my transcribed videos in my library. from VidNotes web.  ←───┘
```

Nothing is lost or duplicated, which is why it never reads as a failure — it reads as though you dictated a sentence in the wrong order. Pacing the events apart was tried first and shipped, and the failure came straight back on the next long transcript; ordering across separately posted events is not something the API offers, so spacing them can lower the odds and no more.

Ara now pastes instead. Anything over 100 characters goes through the pasteboard in every app, not just the apps on the built-in list — that list asks which apps mangle typing, when what actually predicts the failure is how much text there is. Both captures were in apps nobody had listed. Short utterances, which are most of dictation and fit in a single event, still type and still leave the pasteboard alone.

**The hotkey you picked did nothing until the next launch.** Picking a key wrote the config file and moved the checkmark; the event tap stayed armed with whatever it started with, and a grey "applies on restart" caption was the only admission. It applies immediately now, including to a hold already in progress, which ends through the ordinary path instead of being stranded.

**Nothing appeared on screen when you started Ara.** The overlay was driven by the keypress handler, so a user who launched Ara and did not press anything saw nothing — and launched from Finder there is nothing else to see, because Ara has no window and no Dock icon. A first run spends over a minute downloading in silence. The warm-up card now shows itself, repaints on every phase, and clears a few seconds after the ready line.

**The overlay was the wrong size, so its own messages were clipped.** "only silence — check your microphone" came out cut off top and bottom, and "no audio captured" broke "captured" across two lines. Not a message-length problem: every state fits the panel with margin. `NSHostingView` reports an intrinsic size that wins over the panel's declared one, so a panel built at 520×84 came up 362×44. Both symptoms follow from that. There is now an automated check that pins the panel's size and every message's fit, which is what was missing when the 44-point panel shipped.

**Cleanup could fall back to plain rules and never say so.** The formatter chain returns text whether it cleaned it or gave up, and the only report was a line on a stderr that a menu-bar user never reads. A fallback now raises a one-time note on the next press. It stays quiet where the rules floor is what you asked for, and where the cause is a standing configuration fault already reported at startup.

**The crash reporter could hang instead of reporting.** It was added in this cycle to catch the audio crash above, and it was built on a mistake: `backtrace_symbols_fd` was treated as safe in a signal handler because it does not allocate. Not allocating is not the same thing — Darwin's implementation takes the dynamic loader's lock — and the handler also built its output out of Swift arrays, which do allocate. A process that faults on corrupted heap is usually holding the malloc lock, so the handler was most likely to wedge in exactly the scenario it existed for, printing nothing and not even dying. Raw frame addresses now go out first through `write` alone; symbolizing follows as best effort under a three-second alarm.

**Two narrower losses found in review.** A single character too large for one keyboard event — a long emoji sequence, a stacked run of combining marks — was truncated by the API; those now paste whatever their length. And a configured `timeoutMs` above 30 seconds could have its rewrite cancelled by the measurement watchdog, throwing away a result that setting explicitly allowed.

## New

**You can dictate before the big model finishes loading.** `whisper-large-v3-turbo` is a 1.6 GB download and a Neural Engine specialisation measured at 149 s on an M3 Pro. Nothing requires the first model loaded to be the chosen one, so if the chosen model has not opened the gate within five seconds, a small multilingual stand-in comes up alongside it and dictation starts on whichever lands first. Measured against an undownloaded 488 MB model: dictation available 17 s after launch, then the chosen model swapped in cleanly when it arrived. A warm start opens in about 6 s and never ladders.

The stand-in is multilingual on purpose. Standing in an English-only model would transcribe a non-English first minute as English nonsense.

**A crash now leaves a backtrace.** This machine writes no crash reports at all — `~/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports` does not exist, and a deliberately faulting binary produced no `.ips` either — and core dumps need a root-writable `/cores`. The only way to see a stack was to attach lldb and wait for it to happen again. Ara now prints one to stderr on SIGSEGV, SIGBUS, SIGILL or SIGFPE and re-raises, so the process still dies of the same signal with the same status. A LaunchAgent install captures stderr to a file.

## Changed

**Two English-only models are gone.** `whisper-base.en` and `whisper-small.en` are no longer selectable. Three sizes was a choice you had to make about a trade-off you could not see, and the reason to pick a tiny model — that a big one took minutes to become usable — is what the warm-up ladder now handles for you. More importantly, both were English-only, and choosing one silently turned language detection off. `whisper-small` is the same weights' multilingual sibling at the same 488 MB and takes the recommendation. Existing installs do not break: an unknown model in your config warns and falls back.

**The daemon says why cleanup was slow.** Three distinct log lines now, because they call for opposite responses: a generation that finished just past its budget, one that never finished at all, and one that ran to its token cap and produced output that gets discarded anyway.

**This repository is the engine and macOS, nothing else.** The iOS work moved out.

## Measured

Local cleanup on near-identical transcript lengths, from one session:

| characters | generation |
|---|---|
| 280 | 5.5 s |
| 396 | 2.3 s |
| 444 | 19.9 s |

That spread is the point. Generation time here is not a function of transcript length, so no per-character budget can separate the fast case from the slow one — which is why 0.3.0 adds diagnostics rather than another budget change. Ara's earlier diagnostic was measuring its own deadline: because the chain cancelled the work at the budget, every overrun in the field read "0.1–0.4 s past its budget" at every budget and every length, and that number had already been used once to double the budget for nothing.

## Still broken

**Local cleanup gives up on long transcripts.** The 19.9 s case above is a small model generating right up to its token cap, and output that hits the cap is discarded by contract — so you get the raw transcript with rule-based cleanup instead. It is unpredictable rather than length-dependent. If it bothers you, `"cleanup": "light"` adds punctuation and tone without rewriting.

**Whisper repeats itself on long audio.** Unchanged from 0.2.0: real captures produced "dark, dark, dark, dark" runs in the raw transcript, before any cleanup touched it.

**Language detection can flip on a long utterance.** Unchanged from 0.2.0.

## Install

Download, open the DMG, drag Ara to Applications. Unsigned, so the first launch needs right-click → Open.

The first run of a large model spends a few minutes compiling it for the Neural Engine. That is once per macOS version, and you can now dictate through most of it.
