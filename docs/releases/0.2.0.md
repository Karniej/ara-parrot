Dictate for a minute and you get every word back. That was not true before this release, in three separate ways.

## Fixed

**Cleanup could stop mid-rewrite and type the result anyway.** The local model had a flat budget of 512 tokens — generous for a sentence, and about two minutes of speech short of enough for a paragraph. When it ran out, generation stopped mid-word and handed back what it had. Nothing downstream could tell that apart from a finished rewrite, so the back half of the utterance was silently dropped at the cursor. The engine now reports why it stopped: a rewrite cut short by the budget is discarded and the raw transcript is typed instead, unpolished and complete. The budget also scales with the transcript now, so ordinary long dictation is cleaned rather than refused.

**The guard that should have caught it accepted a third of the words going missing.** A rewrite was called plausible if it kept 40% of the input, whatever the length. That is the right floor for a sentence — "um so I think maybe we should you know go" losing its filler is an honest 40% cut. It is the wrong floor for a paragraph, and the gap was measured on a real 77-second dictation: **109 words in, 76 out, a ratio of 0.697**. That rewrite had deleted the opening sentence, deleted the closing sentence, and pasted the opening back onto the end. It cleared the old floor with 0.297 to spare.

Filler does not scale — a speaker is not three times as hesitant in a paragraph as in a sentence — while a model's appetite for summarising very much does. The floor now rises with the input: 0.4 up to 20 words, tightening to 0.8 by 100 words, flat above that. When it rejects a rewrite you lose polish, never words.

**Whisper was never told the recording was long.** Decoding options left the chunking strategy unset, so WhisperKit skipped its voice-activity branch and sent multi-window audio down the path its own source comments describe as being for "audio short enough to transcribe in a single window". That is the plain sliding-window loop, where a window failing its thresholds contributes nothing and the words in it are gone. Long audio is now split on silence. Anything under 30 seconds takes exactly the path it always did.

**Nine more apps get pasted into rather than typed into.** Synthesized keystrokes are sent 20 characters at a time and some apps drop or reorder them. Added: Notion, Xcode, Ghostty, Warp, Hyper, Obsidian, Linear, Zed, and Alacritty's neighbours already on the list. A wrong bundle identifier here is inert — it matches nothing and that app keeps typing as before.

## Changed

The portable core moved into its own `AraEngine` target. Nothing user-visible; it is what lets the formatter, dictionary and snippets be shared with other platforms.

## Still broken

**Whisper repeats itself on long audio.** Real captures produced "light, dark, dark, dark, dark, dark, dark, dark, dark, dark" and "because I need them to be closed because I need them to be closed" — in the raw transcript, before any cleanup touched it. Chunking on silence did not stop it. If you hit this, the words are lost before Ara can protect them, and it is the next thing to fix.

**Language detection can flip on a long utterance.** One English recording ended with a hallucinated "Obrigado" and was reported as Portuguese.

## A note on cleanup intensity

If you dictate long paragraphs and want every word kept, set `"cleanup": "light"` in `~/.config/ara/config.json`. Light adds punctuation and tone and rewrites nothing. Medium rewrites, and on a long utterance rewriting is where words go missing — the guard above now catches the worst of it, but declining a rewrite is not the same as getting a good one.

## Install

Download, open the DMG, drag Ara to Applications. Unsigned, so the first launch needs right-click → Open.

The first run of a large model spends a few minutes compiling it for the Neural Engine. That is once per macOS version, and the overlay says so while it happens.
