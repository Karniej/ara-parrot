One fix, reported from use, and it is a good illustration of where the line sits between what a language model can be asked to do and what has to be checked.

## Fixed

**Cleanup no longer types a sentence you did not say.** Dictating "am I running the newest version" came back as "are you running the newest version" — every time, and only with cleanup switched on.

It is not the model answering the question. It is paraphrasing, so the prompt's existing guard — "a question in the transcript is a question to punctuate, not to answer" — never applied. What drives it is the instruction that every transcript needs at least some change: on a sentence that arrives already clean, the cheapest change available is the pronoun.

**Prompt wording does not fix this, and it is worth saying why.** A rule with a worked example was tried first, and measured on the prompt harness at all three intensities. It corrected the exact shape in the example and nothing else: with "am I late" in the prompt, "am I running the newest version" came out right and "should I cancel the meeting or move it" still came back as "should you".

So the check moved to where it does not depend on the model's cooperation. `OutputGuard` already discards rewrites that are implausible — answered rather than rewritten, duplicated, refusals — and falls back to the raw transcript. A person flip is a fourth shape of the same failure, and none of the existing three could see it: the length is right, nothing is duplicated, it is not a refusal, and most of the words are the same. It is simply no longer your sentence.

The check is deterministic and keys on the pair — **first person leaving and second person arriving** — because either alone is a legitimate edit. "I think we should fix the login bug" → "We should fix the login bug" drops the pronoun and adds no "you"; that is tightening, and `high` is licensed to do it. "Are you coming to dinner" is second person because you made it so.

The visible result: the occasional first-person sentence arrives unpolished rather than reattributed. It costs that utterance its cleanup, and it means Ara never types words you did not speak.

The prompt rule stays as well, at `light` and `medium`. It does not generalise, but it reduces how often the guard has to fire, and a rewrite that never flips is better than one caught afterwards.

## Also

**The prompt measurement harness works again.** `scripts/cleanup-eval` copied four sources from paths that moved when the portable engine was split into `AraEngine`. It failed with four `cp: no such file` lines and then produced no prompts — loud, but easy to read past if you are looking at the end of the output for a score. Anything measured with it since that split was measuring nothing.

Harness scores against the pre-fix baseline, on a sentence none of the prompts contain: light 12/15 → 13/15, medium 10/15 → 11/15, high 11/15 → 12/15.
